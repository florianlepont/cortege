## Common facts (read in the sources)

- The IBP v3.2 sheet (CNPF, 02/02/26) lists the thresholds used by the app. Its FAQ says to rely on the IBP thresholds where they differ from the pocket guide (Butler et al. 2024). The IBP counts a tree once per group, at group level, not per type (41 types are only recognition aids). On the tree, observe the trunk and its main sub-vertical divisions; in the crown only the most visible things: crown deadwood, broken main limb or crown top, witches' brooms, vertebrate nests.
- IBP v3.2 thresholds (verbatim substance from the sheet):
  - 6: cime brisee (diameter > 20 cm), or bris de charpentiere au niveau du tronc (surface > 300 cm2 = A5), or fente (width > 1 cm, depth > 10 cm, length > 30 cm).
  - 7: branches ou cime mortes (diameter > 20 cm and length > 50 cm, or diameter > 3 cm and > 20 % of the crown dead).
  - 8: balai de sorciere (> 50 cm) or brogne (with > 5 gourmands).
  - 9: loupes et chancres (diameter > 20 cm).
  - 10: polypore perenne (diameter > 5 cm).
- The pocket guide 2nd edition (2024) has slightly different inventory thresholds: crown dead branches diameter > 10 cm or > 3 cm with > 10 % of the crown dead (IBP: > 20 cm and 50 cm long, or > 20 %); crown top dead diameter > 10 cm at base (IBP sheet: > 20 cm at base). IBP wins.
- Types by group in the pocket guide 2024 (names as printed): group 6 covers Cime brisee, Bris de charpentiere, Fente, Fente causee par la foudre, Fente a l'insertion d'une fourche, plus Entailles de castor (not in the IBP v3.2 group 6 line). Group 7: Branches mortes, Cime morte, Vestige de charpentiere brisee. Group 8: Balai de sorciere, Gourmands ou brogne. Group 9: Loupe, Chancre (the guide 2024 also has Galles, threshold > 20 galles, placed with excrescences but not in the IBP group 9 line). Group 10: Polypore perenne.
- Version note: the file served at the WSL download link is the second revised edition 2024, 64 pages. The vivreencomminges.org PDF is the 2020 first edition; the definitions I used agree between both, differences noted above.

## Group 6: Aubier et bois de coeur apparents

### What it is
Heartwood (and sapwood) laid bare by a break or a splitting of the wood, on a tree that is still alive. The wound edge is surrounded by living wood with flowing sap, so the dead wood is in direct contact with live tissue. The guide's definition is exposure "suite a un eclatement du bois" (breakage or splitting), as opposed to group 5 where only the bark is lost.

### Sub-types (guide names) and thresholds (IBP)
- Cime brisee: trunk broken, tree alive, heartwood exposed under the break; ø > 20 cm at the break.
- Bris de charpentiere (au niveau du tronc, avec bois de coeur apparent): large limb or fork branch broke off, heartwood of the trunk exposed; surface > 300 cm2 (A5).
- Fente: crack through bark and wood; length > 30 cm, width > 1 cm, depth > 10 cm. Variants: fente causee par la foudre (often spiral, with splintered wood, several cracks), fente a l'insertion d'une fourche (length > 30 cm; if one fork branch has fallen, it is a bris de charpentiere).
- Beaver gnawing (Entailles de castor, hourglass shape, > 300 cm2) exists in the 2024 guide group but is not in the IBP v3.2 line for group 6.

### How an observer recognises it
Pale or grey bare wood with a ragged, splintered, torn edge, or a long open slit; often a callus roll of living bark around the edge. Heartwood differs in colour and chemistry from the sapwood next to it. Bats prefer cracks 1 to 5 cm wide, more than 10 cm deep, more than 1 m high (guide).

### Classic confusions
- Group 6 versus group 5 (aubier apparent): 5 is bark loss only (felling or skidding scar, fire scar, rockfall, bark detached forming a shelter or pocket), smooth sapwood, no break. 6 needs a break or a split going into the wood.
- A cut limb: counted in 6 only if cut flush with the trunk and bare surface > 300 cm2; if a stub longer than 50 cm and diameter > 20 cm remains entirely dead, it is group 7; otherwise nothing (IBP FAQ).
- A stump of a coppice stool: counted only if it is part of the living stem with a common base, not if clearly separate at the collar (IBP FAQ).
- A broken main limb that leaves a splintered stub in the crown without touching the trunk: group 7 (Vestige de charpentiere brisee), not 6.

### Current drawing (SVG source and render)
A vertical trunk, a pointed lens-shaped wound with a second nested lens and a thin line inside, six short radial ticks around the inside lens, a few bark dashes. Reading: a bark-loss wound with a scar and concentric callus. Nothing says "break" or "split": no ragged edge, no splinters, no fork or limb stub, no long crack. A forester would read it as group 5 (peeled sapwood) or a healing scar. Misleading by confusion with 5. Verdict: redraw.

### Proposed French definition
Bois de coeur, parfois aussi aubier, mis a nu sur un arbre vivant par une cassure ou un eclatement du bois : cime brisee, grosse branche arrachee au niveau du tronc, ou fente longue. Le bord de la blessure est entoure de bois vivant. Ce n'est pas une simple perte d'ecorce.

### Proposed French confusion bullets
- Seule l'ecorce a disparu, sans cassure ni fente : c'est l'aubier apparent (groupe 5), pas ce groupe.
- Une branche brisee qui laisse un moignon mort dans le houppier, sans toucher le tronc : c'est le bois mort dans le houppier (groupe 7).
- Une branche coupee a ras du tronc compte seulement si la surface sans ecorce depasse 300 cm2 (une feuille A5).

### Drawing brief
Side view of a living trunk (about two thirds of the frame height). Draw a clear break or split, not an oval scar: either (a) a fork with one limb torn away, leaving a ragged, splintered bare surface on the trunk with jagged points and a few long wood fibres, bark edge rolled around it; or (b) a long open vertical crack with irregular jagged lips and, inside, a darker second line. Show the bark of the living trunk continuing around the wound with a thickened callus roll. Add two or three fibre lines inside the bare area to say "wood", and keep proportions that read as large (wound taller than wide for a crack, at least a fifth of trunk width). Avoid the symmetrical lens shape.

## Group 7: Bois mort dans le houppier

### What it is
Dead wood still attached to the living tree, in the crown: dead branches, a dead crown top, or the remains of a broken main limb. It is not the same as dead wood on the ground: it dries and heats up, hosts different species, and cannot be replaced by lying dead wood (guide).

### Sub-types and thresholds
- Branches mortes (in rather shaded crown conditions): IBP: ø > 20 cm and L > 50 cm, or ø > 3 cm and more than 20 % of the crown dead (guide: ø > 10 cm, or ø > 3 cm and > 10 % of the crown).
- Cime morte (crown top entirely dead, wood in the sun): IBP sheet: ø > 20 cm at the base (guide 2024: > 10 cm).
- Vestige de charpentiere brisee: main limb broken, splintered stump remains, trunk not affected; ø > 20 cm at the break, remaining length > 50 cm.

### How an observer recognises it
Stand back and look up: bare branches without leaves (or without needles) in a leafy crown, grey or bark-stripped limbs, a dead spike at the top, or a thick splintered stump. Dead twigs alone do not count: it has to be thick or a large share of the crown.

### Classic confusions
- Group 7 versus 8: dead wood is bare, brittle, leafless; a broom or shoots is living, dense and leafy (or conspicuously dense twigs). A dead broom is not a counted group 8 feature.
- Group 7 versus 6: a break that exposes the trunk wood is 6; a stump or dead limb in the crown that does not affect the trunk is 7.
- A tree that is leafless in winter or in dormancy: check buds and bark before calling branches dead (deciduous trees). Limbs that merely lack leaves on a deciduous tree out of season are not dead wood.
- A dead standing tree is not a living tree with crown deadwood: factor F counts living trees only.

### Current drawing
A trunk ending in a fork, three bare limbs with short side twigs (angular, stick-like, no leaf), and one branch on the right carrying a cloud-shaped leafy mass with small arcs. The dead limbs are acceptable (bare, thin, angular). The cloud is the problem: it can be read as a witches' broom (group 8), a bird nest or a mistletoe clump, and the only living contrast is a single cloud. No bark texture, no thickness cue, no broken stump. Also, branches are thin; the IBP says thick (> 20 cm) or a fifth of the crown. Verdict: needs small fix.

### Proposed French definition
Bois mort encore attache a l'arbre vivant, dans le houppier : grosses branches mortes, cime entierement morte ou reste eclate d'une grosse charpentiere brisee. Il seche et chauffe au soleil, et abrite d'autres especes que le bois mort au sol.

### Proposed French confusion bullets
- Une cime brisee qui expose le bois du tronc vivant est classee dans l'aubier et le bois de coeur apparents (groupe 6).
- Un arbre sans feuilles en hiver n'a pas forcement de bois mort : verifier les bourgeons et l'ecorce.
- Les petites brindilles mortes isolees ne comptent pas : il faut de grosses branches ou plus d'un cinquieme du houppier mort.

### Drawing brief
Crown seen from a distance, trunk entering a spreading crown. Left half: a living crown with leafy outline (broad rounded lobes, no inner clutter). Right half and top: dead limbs drawn as thick, leafless, pale branches, with bark partly flaking (a few short strokes), broken tips and one thick splintered stump at the top (dead crown top). Keep the dead limbs clearly thicker than the twigs and make the contrast living versus dead readable. Remove the isolated cloud on one branch.

## Group 8: Agglomerations de gourmands ou de rameaux

### What it is
A dense tangle of shoots growing out of one point: either a balai de sorcière (a ball of twigs on a branch, many fine shoots growing from dormant buds, often caused by a rust fungus, mite, phytoplasma or a mutation) or a brogne (dense group of gourmands, epicormic shoots from dormant buds under the bark, on the trunk). The guide notes small thrushes, wrens and even buzzards may nest in them.

### Sub-types and thresholds
- Balai de sorcière: dense cluster of twigs on a branch; IBP: > 50 cm (diameter of the ball).
- Gourmands ou brogne: dense tuft of gourmands on the trunk; more than 5 shoots.

### How an observer recognises it
Balai: a dense, bushy, ball or broom-shaped clump hanging in the crown, visible mainly with a crown view or in winter, with many short twigs and no regular branching. Brogne: a tuft or sleeve of many thin shoots straight out of the trunk bark, often at a knotty swelling.

### Classic confusions
- Mistletoe (group 12 gui): mistletoe balls are round, evergreen and made by the parasite itself, with forked yellowish-green stems and leathery paired leaves; a balai is made by the tree. In winter on a deciduous tree a mistletoe is easy to mistake for a broom.
- Crown deadwood (7): a dead twig tangle is not a counted broom; the broom is living, leafy shoots.
- A few isolated gourmands (fewer than six) do not count.
- Galls (small deformations of leaves or buds) are a separate type; not a broom.

### Current drawing
Trunk with a sunken oval at the base of the shoot fan, eight stick shoots radiating up and out, each ending in a small oval leaf, two shoots exiting through the trunk outline. It reads as a flower-like fan of eight sticks with a leaf each, or a bouquet; the threshold > 5 is shown (8) which is right for a brogne. Missing: density (it should be a tangle, not eight clean lines), the balai de sorciere (the other half of the group) is absent, and the oval at the base is ambiguous (looks like a hole or burl). The two outliers at the trunk edge cross the outline, which is fine for epicormic shoots. Verdict: needs small fix.

### Proposed French definition
Amas dense de jeunes pousses sortant d'un meme point : soit un balai de sorciere, boule de rameaux serres sur une branche (plus de 50 cm), soit une brogne, touffe de plus de cinq gourmands sur le tronc. Ces pousses sortent de bourgeons dormants de l'arbre.

### Proposed French confusion bullets
- Le gui forme aussi des boules dans le houppier, mais c'est une plante parasite (groupe 12) et ses tiges sont fourchues, avec des feuilles epaisses.
- Quelques gourmands isoles, moins de six, ne comptent pas.
- Un enchevetrement de rameaux morts est du bois mort dans le houppier (groupe 7).

### Drawing brief
Two small views in the same frame. Left: a brogne on a vertical trunk, a swelling at the base of a dense bundle of 10 to 15 short thin shoots with small leaves, overlapping, starting from the same spot and crossing the trunk outline. Right: a branch from the crown seen from below, carrying a dense ball of 40 to 60 tangled twigs, irregular scribble fill inside a rounded silhouette much thicker than the branch. Show size by drawing the ball at least three times the branch thickness. Leave the base oval out.

## Group 9: Loupes et chancres

### What it is
Excrescences of the trunk in the shape of a rounded ball of wood more or less compact.
- Loupe: proliferation of cells (tree stressed by wounds, fungi, bacteria, viruses), with rough bark; the wood is not rotten and the bark stays more or less intact.
- Chancre: a necrotic wound (often caused by a fungus or bacterium, for example Nectria or the rust Melampsorella) with decayed wood and exposed sapwood; the bark opens, cracks or lifts, sometimes the callus forms concentric rolls.

### Thresholds
IBP: ø > 20 cm for both (the guide adds, for chancre: or a large part of the trunk covered).

### How an observer recognises it
Loupe: a rounded, smooth to rough-barked bulge, like a tumour on the stem profile, wood sound. Chancre: an irregular wound with bark opened or sunken, often with concentric ridges around a dark or rotten centre, cracked bark, sometimes exposed wood or fungal growth.

### Classic confusions
- Loupe versus chancre: loupe wood is alive and bark intact; chancre wood is dead and decaying, bark open. Wikipedia fr notes that in everyday use loupe and broussin are used as synonyms and both often confused with chancre; loupes rarely endanger the tree, a chancre can.
- Loupe versus broussin: a broussin is epicormic, bristling with shoots (that goes in group 8 when more than 5 shoots), a loupe has a smoother, veined structure.
- Loupe versus gall (guide type Galles, threshold > 20 galls): galls are small parasite-induced deformations on leaves, twigs or buds, not trunk balls.
- Loupe versus cavity: a loupe is a bulge, a cavity (group 2) is a hole.

### Current drawing
Two items. Left: a hatched oval patch on the trunk face with a small hook at the top, reads as a flat pad or plate, not a swelling or a sunken wound; the hatching direction is unexplained. Right: a trunk profile bulge with two inner arcs, reads as a loupe seen in profile (good). Because the left one does not read as a canker (no concentric callus, no cracked bark, no exposed wood, no sunken centre), a forester would not know what it is. The two objects are not labelled as loupe and chancre, so the pair ought to be clearer. Verdict: needs small fix; redraw the canker.

### Proposed French definition
Excroissance du tronc de plus de 20 cm. La loupe est un renflement arrondi d'ecorce rugueuse sur un bois sain. Le chancre est une plaie malade, ecorce ouverte ou craquelee, bois carie et aubier visible, souvent entouree de bourrelets.

### Proposed French confusion bullets
- Loupe : le bois reste vivant et l'ecorce intacte. Chancre : le bois est mort et carie, l'ecorce est ouverte.
- Une grosse touffe de pousses serrees sur le tronc est une brogne (groupe 8), pas une loupe.
- Un creux dans le tronc est une cavite (groupe 2) : une loupe fait une bosse, pas un trou.

### Drawing brief
Two panels side by side on the same trunk silhouette. Left, the loupe in profile: a round, regular bulge protruding about a fifth of trunk width, with the bark continuing over it, short rough-bark strokes and veins following the curve. Right, the chancre seen from the front: an irregular oval plate, sunken, with three or four concentric callus rolls (thick outline then inner outlines), the centre filled with dark hatching or a rough crack texture, a small ragged bark lip at the edge. Label by contrast: bulge versus sunken. Keep both larger than a third of the trunk width so the 20 cm threshold reads.

## Group 10: Sporophores de champignons perennes

### What it is
Fruiting bodies of wood-rotting polypores that live several years: tough, woody (sub-woody) texture, adding a new tube layer each year (several superposed tube layers if more than one year old). Ex: Fomes fomentarius (tinder fungus) hosts about 600 arthropod species in Europe (guide). Woodpeckers sometimes excavate right below it because the wood is soft and the fungus shelters the entrance.

### Threshold
IBP: polypore perenne, ø > 5 cm. (Not the group 11 threshold of 5 cm or more than 10 annual polypores.)

### How an observer recognises it
Hard, hoof or shelf-shaped brackets sticking out of the trunk, grey, brown or black crusty upper surface with concentric growth ridges and a flat, pored (tubes) underside; cannot be squashed by hand; remain in winter and in all seasons; the same bracket gets thicker year by year.

### Classic confusions
- 10 versus 11: annual polypores have only one tube layer and a soft, elastic or leathery texture (several weeks to one season); fleshy gilled mushrooms (Agaricales) are also group 11; a very large single bracket can be annual (Laetiporus is annual); check hardness and layers.
- Fruiting bodies growing on a patch of bare sapwood count in both groups (6 or 5 and 10), as the IBP FAQ says: a tree is counted once per group represented, even when features overlap.
- Pyrenomycetes (hard black crusts, hemispherical) are group 11 per the guide, not 10.

### Current drawing
Three half-hoof shelves on a trunk, two with a couple of growth lines, one small without lines, flat undersides, with a vertical attachment edge. Clearly bracket fungi; reads as polypores. Missing perennial cues: thickness (they look thin, like annual shelves), repeated concentric ridges (only one or two lines), hoof profile (the Fomes shape: thick base, convex top, under surface sloping), and the strata of tube layers on the edge. A smaller one without lines may suggest an annual. Verdict: needs small fix.

### Proposed French definition
Fructification d'un champignon du bois qui vit plusieurs annees : champignon en console ou en sabot, dur comme du bois, de plus de 5 cm. Il grossit chaque annee et son dessus presente des bourrelets, sa face inferieure a des tubes empiles en plusieurs couches.

### Proposed French confusion bullets
- Un champignon mou ou elastique, avec une seule couche de tubes, qui disparait en quelques semaines est ephemere (groupe 11).
- Un champignon charnu a lamelles est ephemere aussi (groupe 11), meme s'il est gros.
- Un champignon pose sur une zone d'aubier a nu compte aussi dans l'aubier apparent (groupe 5 ou 6) : on compte un arbre par groupe.

### Drawing brief
Two or three hoof-shaped brackets on a trunk, seen in profile. Each: thick (height about half the projection), convex top with five to eight concentric ridges (grooves drawn as parallel curves), flat or slightly sloping underside. On the cut edge of the larger one, draw two or three stacked thin layers (tube strata) to say "several years". One bracket tall and wide, a smaller one above it, so the 5 cm minimum reads. Add a small dark stripe on the underside for the pore surface. Avoid thin leaf-shaped shelves.
