## 11 Champignons ephemeres

What it is: fruiting bodies of wood-rotting fungi that grow for a single year (plus myxomycete plasmodia), lasting at least some weeks, growing on the tree or on dead wood of the tree (trunk, branch, stump, standing or fallen deadwood). Not soil mushrooms. Guide types in this group: polypore annuel (thin, one tube layer, elastic, no woody part), agaricale charnue (thick fleshy gilled fungus on wood), pyreneomycete (hard black hemispherical crust like a piece of coal), myxomycete (gelatinous mobile plasmodium); `32-RESEARCH.md` also lists corticoides.

Thresholds: guide (pragmatic): polypore and agaricale diameter > 5 cm or group of > 10; pyrenomycete > 3 cm or group > 100 cm2; myxomycete > 5 cm. IBP sheet: only polypore annuel and agaricale charnue count, diameter > 5 cm or more than 10 fruiting bodies (per `32-RESEARCH.md`); IBP wins.

Recognition: brackets, shelves or clusters of caps directly on bark or bare wood, on a stem, a dead branch, a stump or a fallen log. Soft or leathery, not woody.

Confusions:
- Group 10 (perennial fungi): hard, woody, multi-layered (tinder fungus, Fomes) that persists for years; a thin elastic annual bracket is group 11.
- Mushrooms on the ground (soil mycorrhizal fungi) are not a DMH at all.
- Mosses, lichens or leafy bark growths are group 12, not fungi.

Current drawing: three caps with stems on a wavy ground line, with dots below as soil. This is wrong: it shows soil mushrooms. A forester would read it as "champignons du sol". It is also silent about the wood. Note for the owner: the mushroom form (cap on a stem) is acceptable for agaricale charnue but the substrate must be wood.

French proposals:
- Definition: "Fructifications de champignons qui vivent dans le bois et ne poussent qu'une seule année : petits polypores souples en console, gros champignons charnus à lamelles sur le bois, ou masses gélatineuses de myxomycètes. Elles tiennent au moins quelques semaines sur le tronc, une branche, une souche ou du bois mort."
- Confusions:
  - Un polypore dur, ligneux, qui dure des années est un champignon pérenne (groupe 10).
  - Un champignon posé sur le sol n'est pas un dendromicrohabitat.
  - Mousses et lichens sur l'écorce relèvent du groupe 12.
- Drawing brief: viewpoint side view of a dead trunk or a stump cut off at mid height, bark texture lines. On the wood: a stacked group of three or four thin semicircular brackets (annual polypore, tiers staggered up the trunk, with fine growth rings on top), and one fleshy cap with gills attached directly to the side of the wood by a short stub. Ground line only at the bottom, with nothing growing from it. Optional small jelly blob (myxomycete) on a log in the corner, not needed.

## 12 Plantes et lichens epiphytiques ou parasites

What it is: vascular plants, mosses or lichens that use the tree mainly as a support (epiphytes) or partly feed on it (semi-parasite). Guide types: bryophyte (mousse ou hepatique), lichen foliace ou fruticuleux, lierre ou liane (lierre, clematite), fougere (growing on the trunk or in a branch fork), gui (hemiparasite, usually in the crown: Viscum, Loranthus, Arceuthobium).

Thresholds: guide (pragmatic): mosses > 10 % of the trunk covered; lichens > 10 % of the trunk and thickness > 1 cm; ivy or lianas > 10 % of the trunk; ferns > 5 fronds; mistletoe diameter > 20 cm (Viscum, Loranthus) or > 10 clumps (Arceuthobium). IBP (per `32-RESEARCH.md`): > 20 % of the trunk for at least one of mosses, lichens, ivy or lianas, ferns > 5 fronds (v3.2 only), mistletoe 10 balls of diameter > 20 cm; IBP wins.

Recognition: a green or grey carpet on the bark, grey-green leafy lobes or small shrubby tufts, ivy stems climbing the trunk, ferns tufts in a fork, round evergreen balls high in the crown.

Confusions:
- Mistletoe is a plant living on the tree (group 12), not a deadwood or growth feature such as a witches' broom (group 8, which can look like a ball in the crown).
- Green algae or a simple dirty film are not mosses.
- Fungi on the tree are group 10 or 11, not 12.
- A plant rooted in soil and only leaning on the tree is not an epiphyte; ivy rooted in the ground and climbing counts as a liana.

Current drawing: a trunk with a climbing stem with leaves (ivy), a cloud-shaped patch with dots (moss or lichen), and a crown-side branch ending in a wheel (the mistletoe). Content is correct and covers the main types, but: the wheel with spokes looks like a bicycle wheel or a sun, not a mistletoe ball; the lichen cloud with four dots is ambiguous (could be a growth or fungus); no fern. A forester would probably get ivy and moss, not mistletoe. Verdict small fix: redraw the mistletoe as a bush of forked branches ending in paired leaves and berries, hanging from a branch; add a textured outline for lichen (lobes or tufts) and a small fern at a fork if space allows.

French proposals:
- Definition: "Plantes, mousses et lichens qui utilisent l'arbre surtout comme support : mousses et lichens qui couvrent l'écorce, lierre ou autres lianes qui montent sur le tronc, fougères installées sur le tronc ou à une fourche, et gui, touffe verte arrondie dans le houppier qui puise un peu de sève de l'arbre."
- Confusions:
  - Le gui est une plante qui vit sur l'arbre (groupe 12), à ne pas confondre avec un balai de sorcière (groupe 8), qui est une touffe de rameaux de l'arbre lui-même.
  - Une pellicule verte ou sale sans relief n'est pas une mousse.
  - Les champignons, même sur lichens, ne comptent pas ici (groupes 10 et 11).
- Drawing brief: side view of a trunk with a branch fork in the upper part. Bark with a wide patch of moss (small soft tufts) and a patch of leafy lichen (flat rounded lobes with curled edges). Ivy stem climbing the trunk with five or six lobed leaves. At the fork a small fern with arching fronds. In the crown, a round mistletoe bush hanging from a branch: forked stems, paired oval leaves and a few small berries.

## 13 Nids

What it is: nests of vertebrates (bird, rodent) or invertebrates placed on a part of the tree or sheltered in a cavity. Woodpecker excavations (group 1) are excluded. Guide types: nid de vertebre (diameter > 10 cm), nid d'invertebre (presence: caterpillar nests, ant or wild bee colonies, processionary caterpillar web).

Thresholds: guide: vertebrate nest diameter > 10 cm; invertebrate nest presence. IBP (per `32-RESEARCH.md`): only a large vertebrate nest, diameter > 50 cm; IBP wins. In practice that is a big raptor or heron or crow-group stick nest.

Recognition: a platform or ball of sticks in a fork, against the trunk or in a crown, often seen from below; a squirrel drey is a leafy ball of twigs.

Confusions:
- A nest in a cavity or woodpecker hole is counted in the cavity groups, not here.
- A witches' broom (group 8) or a mistletoe ball (group 12) looks like a nest from the ground; a nest has sticks of different directions and a hollow top.
- A crown microsoil (group 14) is soil held in a fork; a nest is built material.

Current drawing: a trunk splitting into two limbs holding a wide shallow bowl of stick lines with two egg-like ovals. Correct for a large nest in a fork and readable; a forester would recognise it. Weak points: the limbs are drawn very thin and stay under the nest as a "stand"; no scale cue (nest vs fork); the eggs say "bird" but the 50 cm threshold is invisible. Verdict small fix: make the nest clearly large relative to the limbs, with the sticks projecting outward at the rim, and keep the eggs optional. The drawing fits both a vertebrate nest and the 10 cm guide thresholds.

French proposals:
- Definition: "Gros nid d'oiseau ou de rongeur posé dans une fourche ou contre le tronc, fait de branchages. Pour l'indice de biodiversité potentielle, seul un grand nid de vertébré d'environ 50 cm de diamètre compte. Les loges de pic ne sont pas des nids ici."
- Confusions:
  - Une loge de pic ou une cavité occupée se note avec les cavités, pas ici.
  - Un balai de sorcière ou une touffe de gui, vus d'en bas, ressemblent à un nid ; un nid a un creux et des brindilles dans tous les sens.
  - Un peu de terre prise dans une fourche est un microsol (groupe 14), pas un nid.
- Drawing brief: view from slightly below, a trunk forking into two thick limbs; in the fork a large bowl-shaped nest of crossing sticks with ends sticking out of the rim, wider than the limb diameter; a small hollow on top with one or two eggs visible. A human-size scale mark is not needed. Optional: tiny side detail of a caterpillar web for the invertebrate type, outside the main picture.

## 14 Microsols

What it is: a small volume of young soil formed by humification of organic debris (twigs, leaves, bark, moss) on the tree. Guide types: microsol d'ecorce (on the trunk bark, from moss, lichen and algae residue and old thick bark), microsol du houppier (in the crown: flat areas, forks, junctions of fused trees; often penetrated by the tree's own fine roots). Presence is enough (no size threshold). Also an intermediate "microsol d'entre-ecorce" appears in `32-RESEARCH.md` (not seen in the guide text I read).

Thresholds: guide: presence (pragmatic). IBP (per `32-RESEARCH.md`): crown microsoil at any height of the tree; IBP is narrower than the guide (bark microsoils not scored).

Recognition: dark, crumbly, humus-like soil sitting in a fork, hollow or flat area of a branch, often with moss, small plants or fine roots. Typical on old beech.

Confusions:
- Deadwood-cavity "terreau" in a trunk or base cavity (group 2) is rotten wood inside a cavity; a microsoil is humus on the outside surface or in a fork.
- A nest (group 13) is stick-built material; a microsoil is soil.
- Plants growing in it (group 12) are the occupants, not the microsoil.

Current drawing: a V-shaped fork with a dotted wedge at the bottom, a seedling with two leaves on a dome. As an idea it is right (soil pile in a fork) but misleading: the fork looks like a trunk with two arms and a small plant, "germination in a V" rather than "crown humus", the dots do not read as soil, and the walls are thin lines that make it look like a funnel. Verdict redraw with a fork of two thick limbs that clearly belong to a large tree, a dark filled-by-hatching soil mound resting in the angle, moss at the edge and a few fine roots going into it.

French proposals:
- Definition: "Petite couche de terre qui se forme sur l'arbre lui-même, à partir de brindilles, de feuilles, d'écorce et de mousses décomposées. On la trouve surtout dans le houppier, dans une fourche ou sur une zone plate d'une grosse branche, et parfois sur l'écorce du tronc. Des racines fines de l'arbre y pénètrent souvent."
- Confusions:
  - Le terreau au fond d'une cavité ou d'un pied creux est du bois décomposé dans une cavité (groupe 2).
  - Un nid est fait de branchages posés, pas de terre (groupe 13).
  - Les plantes qui poussent dans ce sol se notent à part (groupe 12).
- Drawing brief: view from below, the crown of an old broad tree; a thick fork of two limbs in the upper part. In the angle of the fork a mound of dark soil (dense short hatching and crumbs), with a tuft of moss on its edge and a few fine roots going down into it from the bark. A leaf litter detail (a few dead leaves) at the surface. No seedling needed.

## 15 Coulees de seve et de resine

What it is: active flows of sap (deciduous trees, for example oak, birch, maple, a wound or crack leaking) or resin (conifers, thick sticky flow) on the bark or wood. Guide types: coulee de seve (active flow), coulee de resine (active flow). Resin forms an antimicrobial barrier; sap is attractive to adult insects.

Thresholds: guide (pragmatic): length > 10 cm for both. IBP (per `32-RESEARCH.md`): flow > 20 cm; active sap, fresh or old resin in v3.2; v3.0 does not count resin (hence the v3.0 override in the plan). IBP wins.

Recognition: a dark wet streak or a glossy or frothy run below a wound, a crack or a branch stub; for resin, a pale yellow, amber or whitish sticky mass, hardening, on a conifer.

Confusions:
- Sap flow versus resin: sap runs on broadleaves (wet, darkening, often with flies and ants), resin on conifers (viscous, pale, crusting). In v3.0 only sap counts.
- A dry old exudate is not an active flow (except old resin in v3.2).
- Rain stains or algae streaks are not sap; the flow starts at a wound or crack.
- Bleeding after a fall or storm break is a wound (group 6) with a flow on top.

Current drawing: a trunk with an oval wound near the top and three vertical lines ending in droplets. Good for sap (runs from a wound, drops at the end), readable and honest. Missing: nothing says the flow is long (> 20 cm needed), nothing shows resin. Verdict small fix: make the streaks clearly longer than the wound (they already are), add a short comment-free variant or a second element on the right: a thick pale blob of resin hanging from the wound on a conifer-style bark (scale bark lines), without making the sap look like a drip.

French proposals:
- Definition: "Écoulement actif de sève sur un feuillu, ou de résine sur un conifère, qui part d'une blessure ou d'une fente et descend le long de l'écorce. Il doit mesurer au moins 20 cm de long pour l'indice de biodiversité potentielle. La sève est liquide et sombre, la résine est épaisse et collante, jaune ou blanchâtre."
- Confusions:
  - La sève (feuillus) et la résine (conifères) ne s'écoulent pas de la même façon ; dans l'ancienne version de la méthode, seule la sève compte.
  - Une trace sèche ou ancienne sans écoulement n'est pas une coulée active (sauf résine ancienne dans la version 3.2).
  - Une trace de pluie ou d'algues ne part pas d'une blessure.
- Drawing brief: side view of a trunk; on the left a broadleaf bark with a crack from which a long dark wet streak runs down at least four times the height of the wound, ending in a drop and with a couple of small flies. On the right (second panel or second trunk), a conifer trunk with scale-like bark and a thick resin mass hanging from the wound, with a thick bead at the end. Keep the wound shape asymmetric, not a perfect oval.
