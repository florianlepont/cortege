# Verification of DMH groups 01 to 05 (IBP FR v3.2) and of their line drawings

Date: 2026-10-26. Scope: groups 1 Loges de pic, 2 Cavites a terreau, 3 Orifices et galeries d'insectes, 4 Concavites, 5 Aubier apparent.
Method: the WSL pocket guide 2nd edition 2024 (French) and the official CNPF IBP FR v3.2 sheet were read in full text; the PNG renders and the SVG sources of our five drawings were inspected; a few guide pages were also rendered and looked at (photos of groups 2, 3 and 4).

Where the IBP sheet and the pocket guide differ, the IBP value wins (as asked). Differences found:

| Item | IBP FR v3.2 sheet | Pocket guide 2024 |
|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Bois sans ecorce (group 5) | S > 600 cm2 (A4) | S > 300 cm2 (A5) |
| Orifices d'insectes (group 3) | hole diameter > 2 cm only | hole > 2 cm OR many smaller holes over > 300 cm2 |
| Grouping | 15 groups, 41 types in the IBP sheet | 47 types in Larrieu 2018 / 2024 guide numbering 1 to 47 |
| Loge de pic, medium entrance | 4 to 7 cm, large > 10 cm (gap 7 to 10 cm, both documents) | same |

The IBP counts living trees only, observes the trunk and its main sub-vertical divisions (crown only for a few conspicuous items), counts a tree once per group, and caps at 2 trees/ha per group. It is a quick walk-through, not an inventory: the observer must still step closer to confirm a feature.

## Group 1: Loges de pic (woodpecker breeding cavities)

**What it is.** A nesting cavity dug by a woodpecker (a round entrance, a chamber inside the wood). It stays in this group whoever uses it later. IBP types: small loge (entrance < 4 cm, typically small woodpeckers, often in a dead branch), medium (4 to 7 cm, great spotted/middle spotted/green woodpeckers, usually in decaying wood, dead branch, snag, old branch scar), large (> 10 cm, oval, black woodpecker, usually on a branch-free stretch of trunk), and the "flute" (at least 3 loges aligned on the trunk, each entrance > 3 cm, at most 2 m between two successive ones).
**Recognise it.** Clean, regular, round (or tall oval for the black woodpecker) entrance with a sharp edge, often with a dark interior; on oak a trunk loge is almost always linked to wood-rotting fungi (guide, "bon a savoir").
**Frequent confusions.** (a) Feeding hole of a woodpecker: a conical dig, wider than deep, no chamber: it is group 4 (Concavites), not group 1. (b) Rot cavity with mulm: irregular opening, no clean round edge: group 2. (c) Insect exit holes: small, scattered, no chamber: group 3.

**Our drawing (dmh_01).** Two ovals on a straight trunk, the bigger one with two arcs inside. The arcs read as a face with closed eyelids or a smile; the interior is not shown dark; there is no rim, no sense of depth. Sizes (one big, one smaller) are fine. **Verdict: needs small fix** (redraw the interior and remove the arcs; keep the two sizes; optionally add a third loge to hint at the flute).

**Proposal (French).**
- Definition: "Une loge de pic est un trou rond, net, creusé par un pic pour nicher dans un tronc ou une grosse branche. Elle compte même si un autre animal l'occupe aujourd'hui. Le guide de poche distingue les petites loges (moins de 4 cm), les moyennes (4 à 7 cm), les grandes (plus de 10 cm) et la flûte de pic (au moins trois loges alignées)."
- Confusions:
  - Un trou en forme d'entonnoir peu profond, fait par un pic pour se nourrir, n'est pas une loge : c'est une concavité.
  - Une ouverture irrégulière avec du terreau à l'intérieur est une cavité à terreau.
  - De petits trous d'insectes dispersés ne sont pas des loges.
- Drawing brief: Trunk seen from the front, bark dashes. One large oval entrance (tall, about 90 x 130 px) filled dark, with a slightly thicker lighter rim and a hint of wood chips at the base; one small round entrance (about 40 px) filled dark lower on the trunk; optional third small hole above to suggest a flute (three in a line). No arcs inside the holes. Round, regular edges are the key clue.

## Group 2: Cavites a terreau (rot / mulm cavities)

**What it is.** A cavity in the wood that contains mulm: the brown, crumbly mix of decayed wood, animal remains and droppings. It is protected from rain by a ceiling. IBP threshold: opening > 10 cm, or > 30 cm for semi-open and open types. Types (guide numbering 5 to 10): de pied (floor in contact with the ground, the opening may still be high on the trunk), de tronc (floor not touching the ground), semi-ouverte (rain can enter, > 30 cm), ouverte vers le haut or "cheminee" (after a trunk break, with or without ground contact, > 30 cm), branche creuse (broken big branch forming a tube, > 10 cm). The guide describes five stages of mulm development, from decaying wood to fine soil-like mulm. Cavities that start as a large wound often end here (guide, group 16 note: a big unhealed bark wound eventually becomes a cavity with mulm).
**Recognise it.** Hollow in the trunk or at the foot, with a roof and dark interior; mulm visible at the opening or lying on the floor; dark stains or lichens on the bark below it. The size is bigger than a woodpecker hole and the edges are irregular.
**Frequent confusions.** (a) Root concavity with a hard floor and no mulm: group 4; the guide says explicitly that if mulm is present, it is a "cavite a terreau de pied". (b) Dendrotelme (water-holding fork cup) becomes a cavite a terreau when the wood is decayed (IBP FAQ). (c) Woodpecker loge: small, round, clean. (d) Plain soil or leaf litter piled at the foot is not mulm.

**Our drawing (dmh_02).** A tall arched opening (a double arch, like a tunnel or a church door) at the foot of the trunk, with 11 dots at the bottom and a small arc. It reads as a doorway or a mouse hole. The dots are not clearly mulm; nothing shows that there is a ceiling, a depth, or a cavity inside a hollow trunk; the ground line is barely marked; nothing separates it from a root concavity (group 4). **Verdict: redraw.**

**Proposal (French).**
- Definition: "Une cavité à terreau est un creux dans le bois, abrité de la pluie par un plafond, qui contient du terreau : une matière brune et friable faite de bois décomposé, de déjections et de restes d'animaux. Elle compte à partir de 10 cm d'ouverture (30 cm si elle est semi-ouverte ou ouverte vers le haut). Elle peut être au pied de l'arbre, dans le tronc ou dans une grosse branche creuse."
- Confusions:
  - Un creux au pied avec un fond dur et sans terreau est une concavité racinaire, pas une cavité à terreau.
  - Un simple tas de terre ou de feuilles au pied de l'arbre n'est pas du terreau.
  - Une loge de pic est un trou rond et net, sans terreau : elle compte à part.
- Drawing brief (800 x 600, two views side by side, no text):
  1. Left (about 40 percent of width): outside view of the lower trunk. An irregular, tall opening with ragged, bulging edges (healed wound lips), the inside filled with dark hatching; a little crumbly mulm spilling out on the ground line; a few short strokes of ground line.
  2. Right: vertical cross-section of the same trunk (a cut-away): thick wall on both sides drawn as double lines, a rounded roof inside, the cavity as a wide empty space; on the floor a heap of mulm drawn as fine stipple and tiny crumb-shaped flakes with a slightly sloping surface; a few crumbs fall through the opening; the dark interior in sparse hatching; ground line at the base. Optionally one larva/beetle silhouette on the mulm.
  What distinguishes it: group 1 has a small, clean round hole and no mulm; group 4 has an open, shallow, bark-lined bowl with a hard floor (no stipple). In group 2 the key elements are the roof, the depth, and the stippled mulm heap.

## Group 3: Orifices et galeries d'insectes

**What it is.** Holes and galleries dug by the larvae of wood-boring (saproxylic) insects. Exit holes betray a network of tunnels in the wood; a gallery is a system of channels and chambers, visible under loose bark or in split wood. IBP threshold: hole diameter > 2 cm (the pocket guide also accepts many smaller holes over more than 300 cm2; the IBP sheet does not, so follow the IBP). One type only.
**Recognise it.** Round or oval holes of at least 2 cm in the bark or wood, often with fine boring dust (frass) around; or, where bark has fallen, engraved patterns on the wood surface: a mother gallery with a comb of larval galleries (bark beetles) or wide winding tunnels (large longhorn or stag-beetle larvae).
**Frequent confusions.** (a) Small woodpecker pecks or loges: woodpecker holes are cleaner and form chambers. (b) Tiny scattered shot-holes under 2 cm do not count under the IBP rule. (c) Fungal rot pockets and bark cracks are not insect holes. (d) A wide gallery system with mulm may be reported as a cavity (group 2).

**Our drawing (dmh_03).** Eight small circles scattered over the trunk, and a rounded-square panel with squiggles. Circles are a fair hint of exit holes (but they are small compared with the 2 cm threshold, so a user may think any pinhole counts). The panel looks like a window or tile, not like bark peeled off; the squiggles do not look like beetle galleries (no mother gallery, no regular comb). **Verdict: needs small fix** (replace the panel by an irregular flap of lifted bark with a recognisable gallery pattern, keep a few larger holes).

**Proposal (French).**
- Definition: "Ce sont des trous de sortie et des galeries creusés dans le bois par des larves d'insectes mangeurs de bois. Un trou compte à partir de 2 cm de diamètre. Les galeries se voient surtout là où l'écorce est tombée : un réseau de couloirs gravés dans le bois."
- Confusions:
  - Des trous minuscules (moins de 2 cm) dispersés ne comptent pas pour cet indice.
  - Un trou net et rond avec une chambre à l'intérieur est plutôt une loge de pic.
  - Une fente ou une poche de pourriture n'est pas un trou d'insecte.
- Drawing brief: Trunk front view. One irregular patch where the bark has dropped (jagged outline, not a rectangle), showing a clear gallery pattern: a straight vertical mother gallery with short, slightly wavy larval galleries radiating out at both sides like a comb, ending in small round pupal chambers. Around it, on intact bark, 4 or 5 round holes clearly larger than pinholes (about 18 to 24 px in a 360 px wide trunk), a few dust dashes under the lowest one.

## Group 4: Concavites

**What it is.** A hole or bowl in the wood, dry or wet, or a sheltered place, without mulm and not dug by an insect. IBP threshold: opening > 10 cm and depth > 10 cm. Types (guide numbering 12 to 15): dendrotelme (cup-shaped hollow that holds rainwater, opening > 15 cm in the guide; bottom and rim can be bark or decaying wood), trou de nourrissage de pic (conical feeding dig, opening wider than the cavity, depth and opening > 10 cm), concavite a fond dur de tronc (natural trunk hollow with hard floor and bark on the inner walls), concavite racinaire (between roots or buttresses and the ground, entrance > 10 cm, depth > 10 cm, roof slope < 45 degrees).
**Recognise it.** A hollow with a hard floor and bark or sound wood on the walls, with no crumbly brown filling. At the foot: a gap between roots. In a fork or on a wide limb: a bowl that holds water and leaf debris.
**Frequent confusions.** (a) Mulm present: group 2 (the guide says so for roots). (b) Nesting hole: group 1. (c) A bowl with decayed wood in a fork is a cavity with mulm (IBP FAQ). (d) A shallow wound with the wood visible but no depth is group 5 or 6.

**Our drawing (dmh_04).** A thick oval opening on the flat side of a vertical trunk, with a second inner outline and two wavy lines at the bottom, that read as a water surface. Water cannot stay in a hole in a vertical face, so a forester will find it wrong; the group's four types are not distinguishable and the picture does not show depth. **Verdict: redraw.**

**Proposal (French).**
- Definition: "Une concavité est un creux ou une cuvette dans le bois, sans terreau, qui n'a pas été creusé par un insecte. Elle compte à partir de 10 cm d'ouverture et de 10 cm de profondeur. Le guide de poche en distingue quatre sortes : le creux qui retient de l'eau (dendrotelme), le trou en entonnoir fait par un pic pour se nourrir, le creux à fond dur dans le tronc et le creux entre les racines."
- Confusions:
  - Si le creux contient du terreau brun et friable, c'est une cavité à terreau.
  - Un trou rond et net, fait par un pic pour nicher, est une loge de pic.
  - Une simple écorce arrachée, sans profondeur, est de l'aubier apparent.
- Drawing brief: Two small vignettes, no text. (1) Fork of the trunk seen slightly from above: an oval basin in the fork with a horizontal water line, a couple of floating leaf strokes, and a thick rim; this is the dendrotelme. (2) Base of the trunk: a wide, low arch formed between two buttress roots, clear light interior, hard floor line, bark continuing on the inner wall, no stipple. Keep both free of mulm so the group is told apart from group 2; optionally add the cone-shaped feeding hole (a wide conical dig, wider at the surface) as a third small element.

## Group 5: Aubier apparent

**What it is.** Wood exposed where the bark has been lost, the sapwood showing. IBP types: bois sans ecorce (bark missing from a surface > 600 cm2, about an A4 sheet; the pocket guide uses 300 cm2, the IBP value wins), blessure due au feu (fire scar, often triangular at the base or cracks higher up, charred wood, > 600 cm2), ecorce decollee formant un abri (opening downwards, shelter for bats) and ecorce decollee formant une poche (opening upwards, collects humus), both with a gap > 1 cm behind the bark, width and height > 10 cm. Over time a large unhealed wound often becomes a cavity with mulm.
**Recognise it.** A patch where the bark is gone, with pale, smooth or silvery wood showing, grain lines visible, and the bark forming a sharp edge around it (callus bulge when healing); or a bark plate standing off the trunk with a gap behind it, or a charred, dark triangular scar.
**Frequent confusions.** (a) A split in the wood or a broken limb exposing heartwood belongs to group 6 (Aubier et bois de coeur apparents); a limb cut flush with the trunk goes to group 6 too (IBP FAQ). (b) A cavity with depth and mulm is group 2. (c) A dead standing tree is not counted (living trees only). (d) A small scrape under about A4 does not count.

**Our drawing (dmh_05).** An elongated rounded shape with vertical lines and two tiny curls at the edges. It reads as a peanut, a loaf or a leaf more than as a bare patch of trunk: bark edges are not shown, nor the bark's thickness, and the vertical lines are generic. Nothing evokes loose bark or fire. The idea is acceptable. **Verdict: needs small fix.**

**Proposal (French).**
- Definition: "L'aubier apparent est du bois mis à nu parce que l'écorce a disparu : écorce arrachée sur une surface d'au moins une feuille A4 (environ 600 cm²), cicatrice de feu, ou écorce décollée qui laisse un vide d'au moins 1 cm derrière elle. Cela ne vaut que pour un arbre vivant."
- Confusions:
  - Une fente du bois ou une branche cassée qui montre le cœur du bois relève du groupe aubier et bois de cœur apparents.
  - Un creux profond avec du terreau est une cavité à terreau.
  - Un petit arrachement plus petit qu'une feuille A4 ne compte pas.
- Drawing brief: Trunk with bark dashes. A tall irregular bare patch (about 90 x 200 px) with a visible thick bark edge all round (double line, small callus bulge at the top), pale wood inside with a few long grain lines. At one side a bark plate lifted off the trunk like a flap (a small gap between flap and trunk, drawn as a short side view or a clear shadow line), and a dark charred triangle at the foot as an option. Keep it light: no filling inside the patch, that is what says "wood".
