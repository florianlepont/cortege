## Root causes found

1. **A border under a gradient.** The forest card clip, the "0 / 50" pill and the flat glass button fallback had `borderWidth: 1` with a translucent hairline on the same view as `experimental_backgroundImage`. React Native 0.86 sizes the gradient to the padding box (inside the border) and repeats it over the whole painting area, so the 1 pt ring showed the next tile of the gradient (its opposite edge colour) under the hairline: darker where the gradient is lit, lighter where it is dark. Fixed with an inset 1 pt box-shadow ring and no border on any gradient view; a gate test (`layers.test.ts`) now fails on the combination.
2. **Circular versus continuous corners.** `borderCurve: "continuous"` only reaches the layers RN shapes through `CALayer.cornerRadius`; outset and inset shadows, translucent hairlines on non-clipping views and the overflow clip use circular arcs. Every glass card and forest card therefore drew a squircle fill under a circular hairline, highlight and shadow cut-out, and the corners did not agree. `borderCurve` was removed from 17 style files (a departure from UI-SPEC line 177, recorded in `12.2-17-FIXES.md`); the same gate forbids it.
3. **An empty `ScreenFrame` native view caused by Fabric view flattening.** The large-title `ScreenFrame` forms a view (background colour, gradient, testID) but not a stacking context, so Fabric hoisted its children into the nearest ancestor and the frame was an empty native view with no subviews. UIKit and react-native-screens follow `subviews[0]` from the screen down to find the scroll view that drives the large title; they stopped in the empty frame, found nothing, and the bar never collapsed or blurred. The first fix (frame holds the scroll view, `contentInsetAdjustmentBehavior` automatic) was correct in the React tree and invisible to the unit tests, which render the React tree and not the native mounting. Found with a view-hierarchy dump in lldb on the simulator; fixed with `collapsable={false}` on the large-title frame (419e12a).
4. **Blur strength.** `systemChromeMaterial` was nearly opaque in light mode and read as a white band; `systemMaterial` blurs and keeps the title readable (ffcbfb9).
